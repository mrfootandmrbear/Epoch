# Morphology card

## Ecological job

Create nursery cover and stabilize sheltered shallow sediment while making the existing coastal-productivity field visible from the shoreline camera.

## Real-world referents

Eelgrass supplies the narrow ribbon silhouette and meadow-scale density. Turtle grass supplies the broader, brighter blades used in the most productive patches. Both remain rooted below the surface rather than forming a floating canopy.

## Silhouette bet

A player should read the low, coherent field of current-leaning ribbons as a calm, shallow lagoon. The meadow should disappear on steep or exposed shore and never resemble terrestrial grass flooded by accident.

## Trait map

| Trait | Environmental driver | Continuous or discrete | Visible expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blade height | Coastal productivity | Continuous | Taller blades in the richest shallows |
| Tuft spread | Gentle substrate | Continuous | Wider, denser patches on low slopes |
| Current lean | Exposure and prevailing water motion | Continuous | Shared directional bend with local phase variation |
| Blade color | Depth and productivity | Continuous | Darker olive at depth, brighter green in productive water |
| LOD geometry | Camera distance | Discrete | Thirteen ribbons nearby, five crossed ribbons far away |

## Family resemblance

Every instance uses the same rooted ribbon grammar, lagoon-green palette, and shared two-segment deformation profile.

## Rejected directions

- Floating leaves or surface canopies, which would confuse seagrass with kelp and obscure the shallow-water visibility win.
- Flower-like ornaments or arbitrary color variants unrelated to depth and productivity.
- Static blades, because current motion is the primary underwater silhouette cue.
