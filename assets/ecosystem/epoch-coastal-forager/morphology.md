# Epoch Coastal Forager morphology card

## Ecological job

This lineage consumes coastal productivity and uses reef structure as shelter, making the already-persistent marine population visible without turning rendered fish into simulation authority.

## Real-world referents

- Wrasse: deep, turning-friendly body and readable paired fins around structure.
- Jack: narrow caudal peduncle, forked tail, and long open-water profile.
- Sea bream: plausible generalist body that can move continuously between those pressures.

## Silhouette bet

At gameplay distance, exposed-water descendants should read long, narrow, and steadily cruising. Reef-associated descendants should read deeper-bodied, broad-finned, and capable of tighter turns. Both retain the same head, tail root, forked caudal fin, and silver coastal palette.

## Trait map

| Trait | Environmental driver | Continuous or discrete | Visible expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body size | Food and energetic reserve | Continuous | Whole-body length and mass |
| Streamlining | Wave exposure/open travel | Continuous | Longer body, reduced depth, narrower paired fins |
| Depth preference | Selected water band | Continuous | Actual placement from surface through benthic water |
| Thermal tolerance | Water temperature | Continuous | Cool-silver to warm-olive lateral palette |
| Maneuverability | Structural complexity | Continuous | Deeper body and larger paired/caudal control surfaces |
| Depth control | Vertical water-column use | Continuous | Dorsal/ventral fin area and vertical swim amplitude |
| Propulsion plan | Ancestry | Fixed for this family | Tail plan; no unsupported paired-fin variant is claimed |
| Energy | Current ecological condition | State, not heredity | Saturation, slight condition scaling, and swim cadence |

## Family resemblance

Every descendant shares one topology, blunt browsing head, narrow tail root, forked caudal fin, lateral stripe, and the same tail-driven swim cycle.

## Rejected directions

- Tropical decorative markings unrelated to habitat pressure.
- Separate collectible species meshes for each trait extreme.
- A land-animal torso rotated into water.
- Per-fish hunger or genetics that would compete with lineage-level authority.

## Runtime motion

`idle` is the stationary authored morph state used by the orthographic previews. `swim` is a procedural tail cycle blending the `swimLeft` and `swimRight` morph targets in the landing renderer. They are runtime states over one export rather than separate skeletal clips.
